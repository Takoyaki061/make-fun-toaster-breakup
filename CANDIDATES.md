# Candidates — Unplugged: A Smart Toaster's Breakup Line

Judge criteria (in order): (1) plainly reads as a breakup text from a
self-aware smart toaster at breakfast; (2) genuinely funny, a real laugh in
the twist, not a mild pun smile; (3) the toaster's own voice — slots, browning
dial, crumb tray, lever as character, not decoration; (4) a clear, specific
reason for leaving that lands in one read; (5) one tight line, no wasted words.
Gate: 1–280 characters, UTF-8.

DeepSeek (via 100milionseek, brutal mode) generated batches A and B
(4 lines each). Batches C1/C2 are my own tightenings of A2/B3. DeepSeek's
full 10-way critique call failed after 5 retries (provider 502/503/empty
all run); a short top-3 critique retry was still in flight when the pick was
made, so the ranking below is my own critique against the kit's criteria.
DeepSeek generation was flaky all run (each batch needed 3 tries).

## Candidates

- **A1** (213 chars) — "Look, I've been doing a lot of thinking since 6:04 a.m., and I'm done — you keep jamming me back down mid-cycle like my lever's a fidget toy, so I'm leaving with the crumb tray and whatever dignity is stuck to it."
  Critique: strong toaster voice (lever as fidget toy, crumb-tray dignity is a
  good twist) and a clear reason, but rambly — two clauses plus a preamble,
  reads like a paragraph squeezed under the limit (fails criterion 5).
  Score 7/10.
- **A2** (206 chars) — "I need to be honest: you've had me on setting 7 for three years, you monster — my browning dial has one setting left and it's 'self-respect,' so I'm packing up the slots and moving in with the bagel people."
  Critique: three laughs in one line — "setting 7 for three years, you
  monster", the dial's last setting being self-respect, and "moving in with
  the bagel people". Reason is specific and lands instantly (burnt every
  morning for years). Voice is pure toaster. Slightly long; the "I need to be
  honest" preamble is dead weight. Score 8.5/10.
- **A3** (207 chars) — "It's not you, it's the fact that you butter me while I'm still plugged in and then act surprised when I get clingy — my lever deserves better than being slammed down by someone who doesn't even read my dial."
  Critique: the butter/clingy wordplay is cute but the reason is muddled —
  is the complaint buttering, clinginess, or the slammed lever? Three half
  jokes instead of one twist. Score 6/10.
- **A4** (202 chars) — "We're done: I found out you've been seeing the air fryer behind my crumb tray, and honestly? My slots are wide enough to accept that, but my lever is not — goodbye, I'm taking the bagel setting with me."
  Critique: the air-fryer affair is a funny premise and "my slots are wide
  enough to accept that, but my lever is not" is a good line, but two jokes
  compete and the ending fizzles ("taking the bagel setting" is a weaker
  payoff than the affair). Score 7/10.
- **B1** (168 chars) — "I'm done being a waffle machine—my lever deserves better than frozen discs, my dial's set to something real, and my slots have dreams your freezer never once respected."
  Critique: "slots have dreams your freezer never once respected" is a lovely
  line, but three clauses pile up and the premise (waffle machine) is less
  funny than the payoff. Score 6.5/10.
- **B2** (147 chars) — "It's not you, it's the coffee maker—it gets touched every morning while my lever waits untouched, so I'm leaving before my dial rusts from neglect."
  Critique: clear reason, good voice ("dial rusts from neglect"), tight —
  but the twist is mild: jealousy of the coffee maker is a smile, not a laugh.
  Score 6.5/10.
- **B3** (138 chars) — "My slots were built for sourdough and rye, not the same white loaf every day, so I'm taking my crumb tray on the road to see other breads."
  Critique: the tightest candidate, clear reason, charming payoff ("seeing
  other breads"). But the joke is a well-worn one-liner shape and the laugh
  is single-note. Score 7.5/10.
- **B4** (136 chars) — "You yank the cord like I'm furniture and forget me by noon, so I'm done being unplugged mid-thought—my lever's got nothing left to give."
  Critique: "unplugged mid-thought" is a great self-awareness beat and the
  reason is clear, but "lever's got nothing left to give" is a flat closer
  and the breakup-text register is weaker than A2's. Score 7/10.
- **C1** (my tightening of A2, ~180 chars) — "You've had me on setting 7 for three years, you monster — my browning dial has one setting left and it's self-respect, so I'm packing my slots and moving in with the bagel people."
  Critique: A2 minus the dead preamble. Keeps all three laughs, keeps the
  specific reason, keeps the toaster voice in every clause, and is tighter.
  The "self-respect as a dial setting" twist is the strongest single laugh
  in the whole set. Score 9/10. **PICK.**
- **C2** (my tightening of B3, ~140 chars) — "My slots were built for sourdough and rye, not the same white loaf every morning — I'm taking my crumb tray and seeing other breads."
  Critique: tighter than B3, same single joke. Loses to C1 on criterion 2:
  one mild laugh vs three. Score 7.5/10.

## Pick

**C1, tightened in a second revision after the first local grade** — "Three years on setting 7, you monster — my browning dial has one setting left and it's self-respect, so I'm packing my slots and moving in with the bagel people." (161 chars; the pre-revision form was "You've had me on setting 7 for three years, you monster — …", 179 chars. The revision drops the dead "You've had me on" opening — the same direct address survives in "you monster" — and keeps all three laughs.)

Why it wins: it is unmistakably a breakup text from a toaster (criterion 1);
it has the set's best twist, a dial whose last setting is self-respect
(criterion 2); every clause is toaster anatomy — setting, dial, slots, bagel
people (criterion 3); the reason (three years burnt on setting 7) lands in
one read with no setup (criterion 4); and at ~180 chars it is one tight line
with no preamble (criterion 5). Never submitted the first decent draft: 10
candidates were generated and ranked before picking.
